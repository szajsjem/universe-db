# Manual propellant research, 2026-10-13

`sources/manual-chemistry/propellants-2026-10-13.json` records six chemical identities and eight qualitative engineering-use facts from directly inspected NIST and NASA-hosted pages. This is model-assisted **unverified research staging**, not reviewed scientific data. Mod usefulness is an authored design rationale, never a measured observation. No density, specific impulse, reaction energy, consumption rate, safety certification or synthesis recipe is inferred.

NIST Chemistry WebBook SRD 69 live identity pages supply formulas; identity-only facts and selected unambiguous aliases are retained. Hydrazine's NIST Hill-order formula H4N2 is written N2H4, with identical composition. No NIST thermochemical tables or spectra are copied. NASA Cassini mission documentation supplies demonstrated flight use; NTRS report identities 19660024164 and 19990062725 supply their stated experimental applications. The NASA-hosted TFAWS06-1026 paper by Roger Herdy is a 2006 public-release DARPA-contract research report; its nitrous-oxide cold-gas and catalytic-monopropellant claims remain candidate uses, distinct from demonstrated nitrous-oxide/propane rocket experiments. URLs identify these live/released source versions and every source records retrieval on 2026-10-13.

Read-only inspection of both reviewed and companion staging databases found no reviewed entity for these six identities. Existing candidate references are recorded per entry: hydrazine (one), dinitrogen tetroxide (one), hydrogen peroxide (two), nitrous oxide (four). Monomethylhydrazine and 1,1-dimethylhydrazine had no matching name or alias candidates. Multiple existing mentions are links to prior research, not new species and not endorsements of their current candidate classifications: in particular one nitrous-oxide mention is incorrectly classified as an element. The new identity proposal is a neutral molecule.

UDMH specifically means **1,1-dimethylhydrazine**, not its 1,2 isomer. Molecular formula alone cannot distinguish those isomers. Nitrogen tetroxide/dinitrogen tetroxide/NTO refer to the same identity here; nitric-oxide-containing MON blends are mixtures and are not aliases. Aerozine-type blends are also mixtures, not UDMH aliases. High-test peroxide is a concentration-defined aqueous formulation, not a second molecular species or an alias of pure hydrogen peroxide. JP-8 is named only as a reported experimental partner; this slice does not propose a fixed formula for that mixture.
